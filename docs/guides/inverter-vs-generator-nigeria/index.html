Inverter system or generator — which costs less in Nigeria?

Guide — HousePlus Group Nigerian Factory

Short answer

For daily household backup, an inverter system with batteries costs far less per kilowatt-hour than a petrol generator — generator fuel alone typically exceeds the amortised cost of a properly sized solar-plus-storage system within two to three years. Generators still win for high-power, occasional loads: welding, borehole pumping several kilowatts, or a workshop of machines running all day.

The fuel arithmetic

A typical 2.5–3kVA petrol generator consumes roughly 1.5–2.5 litres per hour at moderate load. Run it five hours a day and it burns 8–12 litres daily — before oil changes, spark plugs and the inevitable repairs. An inverter system, once installed, consumes nothing to run; its costs are the batteries you eventually replace and the panels that never stop working.

The exact crossover point depends on the fuel price and how many hours a day the grid is absent, which is why we do not publish a fixed payback figure. What is constant is the direction: the more the generator runs, the faster the inverter system pays for itself.

The cheapest way to find out

If you are not sure whether your load needs a wired system at all, start with a portable power station. It is an inverter and a LiFePO4 battery in one box: run your real evening loads from it for a week and you will know your kilowatt-hours to the nearest tenth — the number any quotation depends on — before spending on a whole-house installation.

Where the generator still wins

A generator is the right tool when the load is large and intermittent. A 5kW inverter and the battery bank to feed it for hours costs more than a 5kVA generator by a wide margin — if you only need that power two hours a week, the generator is cheaper and simpler. Workshops, event power and borehole pumping without solar all fall in this camp.

The hybrid answer most homes settle on

In practice, most Nigerian homes that can afford it converge on the same setup: an inverter system with LiFePO4 storage carries lights, fans, electronics, the fridge and Wi-Fi through every outage silently and at no running cost, and a small generator is reserved for the rare heavy or multi-day load. The hybrid 5kW inverter in our catalogue is built for exactly this pattern — grid, solar and generator input on one unit.
