Shipping, delivery & customs

Each product page states its own delivery window — typically 7 to 15 days for small appliances and 10 to 20 days for solar equipment, counted from the deposit clearing. Custom OEM orders take longer because production is scheduled after the artwork is approved. Sea freight to Lagos adds transit time on top of the production window.

Yes. Nigeria is the home market and the main destination, and we ship to other African countries as well as to export markets outside Africa. Air freight is used for small, urgent orders and sea freight for volume. The freight cost depends on weight, volume and destination port.

The buyer's clearing agent normally handles import clearance at destination, and we provide the commercial invoice, packing list and bill of lading needed for it. If you do not have an agent we can recommend partners we have worked with, but the engagement and the duty payment remain between you and the agent.

No. Import duty, value added tax and port charges are payable at destination and are not part of our quotation. They vary by product classification and by country, so your clearing agent is the right source for an exact figure.

Solar panels ship in reinforced cartons on edge, batteries in their UN-rated packaging, and appliances in retail cartons with inner foam. For sea freight we palletise or crate the consignment. Fragile items get extra corner protection because a damaged panel is a total loss.

Yes, for stock items, once the order is paid and ready. Collection avoids freight cost and is often the fastest route for a small order in Lagos. Contact us first so the goods are picked and staged before you arrive.

Warranty, quality & certification

Warranty terms are set per product and are stated on the quotation, because a solar panel, an inverter and an air fryer cannot reasonably share one policy. Solar panels typically carry a long performance warranty plus a shorter product warranty; electronics carry a shorter term. Ask for the written terms for the model you are buying before you order.

Report it with the order number, a photo or video of the fault, and the serial number. If the unit is within warranty we replace or repair it according to the terms for that product. Consumable damage, incorrect installation and lightning damage are normally excluded, which is why correct earthing and surge protection matter.

Certification differs by product line, so the applicable certificates are supplied with the quotation for the specific model rather than claimed as a blanket statement. For regulated categories, ask for the certificate numbers and verify them with the issuing body — a legitimate supplier will always provide them.

Incoming components are checked, finished units are function-tested on the line, and a sample from each batch is held for retest. Batteries are checked for capacity and internal resistance, and inverters are run under load before packing. Test records are kept per batch so a complaint can be traced back to the production run.

Yes. You or a third-party inspection company you appoint may inspect before dispatch. We recommend this on first orders and on any order with custom branding. Book the inspection once production is complete so there is time to correct anything that is found.

Product & technical

One 400W panel is far too small to feed a whole house. A single panel suits a small battery-charging or lighting setup; a 5kW hybrid inverter needs roughly 10 to 13 panels of 400W to reach a sensible array size. The right answer comes from your daily energy consumption in kilowatt-hours, not from the panel count.

It depends on how long you need to run the load. A 5kW inverter on a 48V bus needs at least four 12V batteries in series, or one 48V battery. To run a 3kW load for four hours you need about 12kWh of usable storage, which means roughly three 100Ah 48V batteries, or more once depth-of-discharge limits are applied.

The 100Ah LiFePO4 battery in our catalogue is rated at 4000+ charge cycles, against a few hundred for a lead-acid battery of similar capacity. That cycle count assumes partial discharges and moderate temperature. Discharging to empty every day in a hot room shortens life noticeably.

LiFePO4 costs more up front and far less over its life. It delivers roughly 80 to 90 percent of its rated capacity, tolerates deeper discharge, and lasts several times longer than lead-acid. Lead-acid is cheaper to start with but needs ventilation, regular maintenance and replacement every two to three years in daily cycling.

Expect a large drop. Under heavy cloud a panel may produce 10 to 25 percent of its rated output, and at night none. System design in Nigeria should assume the rainy season delivers several consecutive low-yield days, so either oversize the array or size the battery bank for autonomy across those days.

A 512Wh unit with a 500W pure-sine inverter runs LED lights for many hours, charges phones and laptops repeatedly, and powers a fan, a TV, a router or a small fridge. It will not run an air conditioner, a pumping machine or a welding set — those need a wired inverter system. Charging is from solar panel, wall socket or car, and there is no installation: it works out of the box.

Divide 512Wh by the watts of what you are running. A 10W light runs about 45 hours; a 60W fan about 8 hours; a 100W TV about 5 hours; a phone (20Wh) charges roughly 20 times. The rated 3000+ cycles mean the battery itself holds most of its capacity for years of daily use.
